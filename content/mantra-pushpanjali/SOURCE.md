# SOURCE — मंत्रपुष्पांजली (Mantra Pushpanjali)

- Title: मंत्रपुष्पांजली (मन्त्रपुष्पाञ्जलि)
- Deity: none single — closing Vedic prayer offered with flowers at the end of aartis, to the deity of the puja
- Language: Sanskrit (ancient, Vedic)
- Author: ancient, no human author. The five hymns are drawn from Vedic sources: hymn 1 from Rigveda 1.164.50 (also 10.90.16; Atharvaveda 7.5.1); hymn 2 from Taittiriya Aranyaka 1.31.6; hymns 3–4 from Aitareya Brahmana VIII.15 and VIII.21; hymn 5 from Rigveda 10.81.3. It is an appendix of the Shukla Yajurveda recital called Deve. PD (ancient).
- Size: 5 hymns.

## Sources compared

2. Ganesh.com, “Mantra Pushpanjali with Lyrics & Meaning” — http://www.ganesh.com/ganeshproducts/blog/2017/01/20/mantra-pushpanjali-with-lyrics-meaning-ganpati-mantra-pushpanjali-bhakti-songs/ — full unaccented Devanagari text as recited after aarti. Agrees on all five hymns and their order. Cross-checked against Sonichits' “Mantra Pushpanjali” page (https://sonichits.com/video/Ravindra_Sathe/Mantra_Pushpanjali), which prints the same unaccented wording.

No text, meaning, transliteration, or presentation was copied from any lyric site; the text above was written from the compared wordings.

## Variant readings found and decision taken

- Svara marks: Wikipedia prints the Vedic accents (य॒ज्ञेन॑ etc.); Ganesh.com and the common recited form print the text unaccented. Decision: unaccented plain Devanagari, as recited in aarti practice; the accents are a chanting aid, not part of the words.
- Hymn 3, last word: “एकराळिति” (Wikipedia) vs “एकराट् इति” in some prints. Decision: “एकराळिति”, following Wikipedia's text.
- Hymn 4: “आविक्षितस्य कामप्रेर्विश्वे देवाः सभासद इति” — Wikipedia and Ganesh.com agree; some copies read “कामप्रेः”. Decision: “कामप्रेर्”.
- Hymn 5 (“विश्वतश्चक्षुः”): Wikipedia notes it is “often skipped in currently prevalent recitals of Mantra Pushpanjali performed after Arati”, but that the Deve scriptures include it. Decision: include it, numbered ॥५॥, and note the practice here so a reader who skips it is not surprised.
- Opening “ॐ” and “ॐ स्वस्ति” before hymns 1 and 3 are later additions to the original Vedic text (per Wikipedia); they are kept because they are part of the recited form.
- The common recited form ends with “मंत्रपुष्पांजली समर्पयामि” / “श्रीशुभं भवतु” in some booklets; those are closing utterances, not part of the five hymns, and are not included as text.
