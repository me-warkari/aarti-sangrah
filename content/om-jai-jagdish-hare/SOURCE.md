# SOURCE — ॐ जय जगदीश हरे (Om Jai Jagdish Hare)

- Title: ॐ जय जगदीश हरे / श्री जगदीशजी की आरती
- Deity: Vishnu (Jagdish, Lord of the Universe)
- Language: Hindi (not Marathi — labelled Hindi on the page)
- Author: Pandit Shraddha Ram Phillauri (1837–1881, d. 24 June 1881); composed c. 1870. PD in India (life + 60) and the US (pre-1930).
- Size: refrain + 8 verses + closing couplet.

## Sources compared

1. Ottawa Hindu Temple library, “Om Jai Jagdish Hare — Lyrics in Hindi & English with Meaning” — https://ottawahindutemple.com/library/aarti/om-jai-jagdish-hare — full Devanagari text, 8 verses + closing. Used as the base wording.
2. HinduMirror, “Om Jai Jagdish Hare Aarti Lyrics in Hindi and English with Meaning” — https://hindumirror.com/om-jai-jagdish-hare-lyrics-in-english-jagdish-ji-ki-aarti/ — full Devanagari text, 8 stanzas; agrees on verse order and wording, and confirms the closing couplet as a separate tradition. Cross-checked against JaapDaily (https://jaapdaily.com/learn/aarti/om-jai-jagdish-hare/) for the refrain and closing variants below.

No text, meaning, transliteration, or presentation was copied from any lyric site; the text above was written from the compared wordings of these two sources.

## Variant readings found and decision taken

- Closing couplet, second line: Ottawa reads “कहत शिवानन्द स्वामी, सुख सम्पत्ति पावे”; JaapDaily and another compared source read “कहत शिवानंद स्वामी, मनवाँछित फल पावे”. Decision: follow Ottawa (“सुख सम्पत्ति पावे”); record the other reading here. The couplet itself is a separate tradition — it names “शिवानन्द स्वामी”, not Phillauri — so it is kept as a closing couplet, not attributed to Phillauri.
- Refrain: some versions (JaapDaily stanza 1) omit the repeated “दास जनों के संकट” line; Ottawa and HinduMirror include it. Decision: include it, as in the most widely sung form.
- “सन्तन की सेवा” (Ottawa) vs “संतन की सेवा” (most other versions): spelling only; kept Ottawa's “सन्तन”.
- Verse count: 8 verses + closing is consistent across both sources; some pages count the refrain as a stanza, giving “9 stanzas” — that is a counting difference, not a text difference.

## Disclaimer

This text is traditional and given as commonly sung. The closing “कहत शिवानन्द स्वामी” couplet comes from a separate tradition and is not part of Phillauri's original composition as far as the sources compared establish; it is included because it is sung with the aarti in most homes.
