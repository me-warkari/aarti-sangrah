# SOURCE — काकड आरती (Kakad Aarti, general Vitthal morning aarti)

- Title: काकड आरती (पहाटेची आरती)
- Deity: Vitthal / Pandurang (Pandharpur)
- Language: Marathi
- Authors (per part, as traditionally credited): Bhupali 1 — Sant Tukaram (1608–1649/50); Bhupali 2 — Sant Janabai (c. 13th–14th c.); Bhupali 3 — Sant Namdev (c. 1270–c. 1350); Kakad Aarti — Sant Tukaram. All PD (died centuries before 1965). The attributions are the traditional ones carried by the aarti collections; where a part is also credited elsewhere, see “Variants”.
- Size: 3 bhupalis (3 + 2 + 5 couplets) + Kakad Aarti (opening couplet + refrain + 3 verses).

## Scope — read this first

“Kakad Aarti” is sung in two overlapping traditions: the general Vitthal (Pandharpur) morning service and the Shirdi Sai Baba morning service, which adopted the Vitthal pieces and added Sai-specific ones. This file gives the **general Vitthal form only — NOT the Sai Baba set**. Omitted, deliberately: Shri Krishna Jogiswar Bhishma's Sai-addressed bhupali (“उठा उठा श्री साईनाथ गुरु …”), his “काकड आरती करितो साईनाथ देवा”, the five-wick-lamp abhang (“घेउनी पंचारती, करू बाबांची आरती”), the Padh (“उठा उठा साधुसंत … उठोनिया पहाटे बाबा उभा असे विटे”), the Sainath Prabhatastak, and “साई रहम नजर करना”. Those belong to the Sai Baba aarti set, which the inventory holds pending per-part verification; they are not included here and their absence is not an error. The one place the two traditions meet in the text — the refrain “ओवाळू आरती माझ्या पंढरीनाथा” — is given here without the Sai version's added “माझ्या साईनाथा”.

## Sources compared

1. TryDiya, “Kakad Aarti Lyrics in English, With Meaning” — https://www.trydiya.com/prayers-mantras/kakad-aarti — full Devanagari text of the Shirdi morning service in sung order (89 passages, including the Sai-specific parts). Used as the base Devanagari wording for the Vitthal parts included here.
2. IndiaDivine / ShirdiBaba.org, “Kakad Arati” (Roman transliteration with English meaning, same service order) — https://www.indiadivine.org/content/topic/1147501-english-translation-of-the-kakaad-aarti/ and https://shirdibaba.org/aartis/aka.htm — agrees on the wording and the part-by-part attributions (Tukaram / Janabai / Bhishma / Namdev) and on the refrain of the Tukaram Kakad Aarti. Used to compare readings where the Devanagari spelling varies.
3. Reference (not a typed source): mr.wikisource.org, “नित्यनेमावली/काकडआरती” — a scan transclusion (pp. 38–42 of R. D. Ranade's नित्यनेमावली, 1987), not typed text; it confirms that the Kakad Aarti sits in the printed Nityanemavali tradition, but its text was not used as a wording source.

No text, meaning, or presentation was copied from any lyric site; the text above was written from the compared wordings.

## Variant readings found and decision taken

- Tukaram Bhupali, line 1: “परिसावी विनंती माझी पंढरीनाथा” (TryDiya Devanagari) vs “Parisawee vinanthee maajzee Sadgurunaathaa” (IndiaDivine / Sai-temple Roman). Decision: “पंढरीनाथा” — the Vitthal form, fitting this file's scope; the Sadgurunatha reading belongs to the Sai service.
- Tukaram Bhupali, “तुका म्हणे देवा माझी वेडीवाकुडी / नामे भवपाश हातीं आपल्या तोडी”: TryDiya reads “तोडी”; some Roman copies read “thondi/thodi”. Decision: “तोडी”.
- Janabai Bhupali: “शुकसनकादिक” (TryDiya) vs “Shuka sanakaadika” (Roman) — same word, spelling only.
- Namdev Bhupali, last couplet: “विष्णुदास नामा उभा घेउनी काकडा” — the signature is Vishnudas Nama, as in the Roman sources; the attribution “Sant Namdev” for this bhupali is the traditional collection credit, and the Vishnudas Nama / Namdev question is the same one recorded for Yuge Atthavis — both traditions are PD either way, and neither is asserted here as settled.
- Tukaram Kakad Aarti refrain: “ओवाळू आरती माझ्या पंढरीनाथा / दोन्ही कर जोडोनी चरणी ठेविला माथा” (this file) vs the Sai service's “माझ्या पंढरीनाथा, माझ्या साईनाथा”. Decision: Vitthal form only, per scope.
- “भक्तीचिया पोटी” (TryDiya) vs “Bhaktaachiya potee” (Roman): spelling only; kept TryDiya's form.

## Disclaimer

This is a composite morning service, not a single author's work: its parts come from different saints and centuries, and the attributions above are the traditional ones, not settled scholarship. It is given here as commonly sung in the Vitthal tradition, with the Sai-specific parts left out as explained above.
